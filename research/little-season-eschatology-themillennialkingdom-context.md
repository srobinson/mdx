# Little Season Eschatology: factual brief

## Executive summary

"Little Season Eschatology" is not a named school of thought in mainstream premillennial scholarship. The phrase appears in two distinct usages: (1) the generic biblical reference to the "little season" of Revelation 20:3, 7-10 when Satan is loosed after the thousand years, treated by every classical premillennial author featured on themillennialkingdom.org.uk as a brief literal future event; and (2) a fringe preterist-adjacent framework promoted by `littleseasonministries.com` and a handful of independent bloggers since roughly 2024-2025, claiming that the millennial kingdom has already occurred and that we are now living in the "little season." The fringe usage is unrelated to the themillennialkingdom.org.uk circle and is rejected by every author featured there.

## Definition and best reconstruction

**Biblical sense (the only sense used by the themillennialkingdom.org.uk authors).** The "little season" (Greek `mikron chronon`, Rev 20:3) is the brief interval after the thousand-year reign of Christ during which Satan is released from the abyss, deceives the nations called Gog and Magog, gathers them against the camp of the saints, and is destroyed by fire from heaven before being cast into the lake of fire (Rev 20:7-10). It immediately precedes the Great White Throne judgment (Rev 20:11-15). Premillennialists treat it as a literal future event of short duration; the Greek noun `chronos` modified by `mikros` denotes a brief but indeterminate span, not a defined period.

**Fringe sense (Little Season Ministries, ca. 2024-2025).** A small but growing online movement teaches that we are *currently* living in the "little season," that the millennium has already happened (variously dated AD 70-1070, 1776-1846, 1945-2015, 1948-2018, or 1950-2020), that mainstream history conceals this prior kingdom, and that the rapture, tribulation, second coming, and millennial reign are all past. This is functionally a hyper-preterist scheme dressed in premillennial vocabulary, often blended with "Tartaria" and "Mud Flood" alternative-history conspiracy theories ([paradigmshyft.com 2025-08-31](https://paradigmshyft.com/2025/08/31/the-millennial-little-season-theory-biblical-prophecy-timeline-shift-the-age-of-deception/), [littleseasonministries.com](https://littleseasonministries.com/), [faithbyreason.net](https://faithbyreason.net/little_season/)). It is rejected as non-orthodox by both Reformed and dispensational commentators ([Convincing Proof](https://convincingproof.org/are-we-living-in-revelation-20/), [Faith by Reason](https://faithbyreason.net/little_season/)).

## Is it a named framework, and who teaches it

There is no "Little Season Eschatology" school in academic theology. As a label it is used by:

- **Little Season Ministries** ([littleseasonministries.com](https://littleseasonministries.com/)) — anonymous, no named founder, teaches that the millennium ran roughly AD 70 to 1070 and that we are now in the post-millennial loosing.
- **Theo Rhising**, *Are We Living In Satan's Little Season?* (2024, ISBN 9781068847875) — independent self-published title on Amazon, the most cited "book length" treatment.
- **Austin Blais**, "The Revealing Biblical Case We Are In The Little Season" ([Medium](https://medium.com/@AustinDBlais/the-revealing-biblical-case-we-are-in-the-little-season-958d7e5047f2)) — independent blogger.
- **King James MD** at [sammutangele.substack.com](https://sammutangele.substack.com/p/the-transition-into-satan-little) — Substack author.
- Various Christian-forum threads and YouTube channels with no institutional affiliation.

None of these teachers are connected to themillennialkingdom.org.uk, Lamp Broadcast, or any classical premillennial publisher. The Christian Study Library and Reformed sources treat the term "little season" purely in its Revelation 20 sense and never as a standalone framework ([christianstudylibrary.org](https://www.christianstudylibrary.org/article/doctrine-last-things-satans-little-season)).

## How the themillennialkingdom.org.uk circle treats Revelation 20:3, 7-10

The site is run by Wilfred H. Tindle (W.H.T.), is explicitly premillennial, and presents itself as a republication archive for classical premillennial writers. Searches against the site and the Lamp Broadcast corpus return no use of "Little Season Eschatology" as a label, and no author featured there treats the little season as a present-tense category.

- **Arlen L. Chitwood (Lamp Broadcast).** Chitwood's books on Revelation, including *The Time of Jacob's Trouble* ([TJT.pdf](https://www.lampbroadcast.org/bookspdf/TJT.pdf)), *Coming in His Kingdom* ([CHK.pdf](https://lampbroadcast.org/Books/CHK.pdf)), *Christians After the Rapture* ([CAR.pdf](https://www.lampbroadcast.org/Books/CAR.pdf)), and *The Rapture and Beyond* ([TRAB.pdf](https://www.lampbroadcast.org/Books/TRAB.pdf)), follow the standard dispensational sequence: rapture, tribulation, second advent, literal thousand-year kingdom, then "events from the middle of chapter twenty to the end of the book" in which Satan is loosed, deceives the nations, and is destroyed by fire from heaven. Chitwood treats the loosing as a brief literal future event, not as a doctrinal category in its own right. He has no book or pamphlet titled around the "little season."
- **D. M. Panton.** Panton's prolific corpus on rapture, kingdom exclusion, and reward (extensively excerpted at themillennialkingdom.org.uk and rationallychristian.org) does not use "little season" as a doctrinal heading. Panton's writings on Christian Responsibility are quoted in the site's outer-darkness compilation ([TheDarknessOfTheOuter.htm](https://www.themillennialkingdom.org.uk/TheDarknessOfTheOuter.htm)), but the focus is reward and exclusion, not the post-millennial loosing.
- **Robert Govett.** Govett is the foundational nineteenth century figure for partial-rapture and millennial-exclusion teaching ([millennialexclusion.com](https://millennialexclusion.com/2022/05/18/the-sermon-on-the-mount-robert-govett/), [zhangdaderen.com PDF](http://www.zhangdaderen.com/THE-MILLENNIUM-AND-ITS-REWARD.pdf)). His framework distinguishes salvation (eternal life, secured by faith) from millennial kingdom participation (dependent on faithful works), and locates "outer darkness" in the millennial age as the realm of excluded but eternally secure believers. The little season of Revelation 20 plays no defining role in his system.
- **G. H. Lang.** Lang's letters quoted on the site confirm the same Govett-Panton framework (kingdom reward and exclusion) without singling out the little season.
- **G. H. Pember** (*Earth's Earliest Ages*, 1876). Pember treats the post-millennial loosing as a literal brief event in continuity with Genesis 6 demonology and Matthew 24, but he does not name it as a distinct doctrinal category.
- **G. N. H. Peters** (*The Theocratic Kingdom*, 1884). The standard nineteenth century systematic treatment of historic premillennialism. Peters discusses the loosing of Satan as proposition 174 ff., treating Gog and Magog literally and the loosing as a future literal post-millennial rebellion with a probative purpose, demonstrating the unregenerate human heart even after a thousand years of righteous rule.

**Verdict:** The themillennialkingdom.org.uk circle has no proprietary "Little Season Eschatology" framework. The site's distinctive contribution is the **kingdom exclusion / outer darkness / reward** doctrine inherited from Govett and Panton, not the little season as such.

## Key scriptures and exegetical moves

- **Revelation 20:1-3** — Satan bound for a thousand years and cast into the abyss; "after that he must be loosed a little season" (`mikron chronon`).
- **Revelation 20:7-10** — At the end of the thousand years Satan is loosed, deceives Gog and Magog from the four quarters of the earth, gathers them against the camp of the saints, and is destroyed by fire from heaven. He is then cast into the lake of fire where the beast and false prophet "are" (present tense after a thousand years of torment).
- **Ezekiel 38-39** — The major Old Testament Gog/Magog passage. Premillennialists are split on whether Ezekiel's Gog/Magog event is the same as Revelation 20:8 (most historic premillennialists, e.g. Peters) or a different earlier event during the tribulation (most dispensationalists, e.g. Chitwood, Walvoord, Pentecost).

**Interpretive moves across the four millennial views:**

| View | Binding of Satan | Millennium | Little season | Gog and Magog |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Dispensational premillennial (Chitwood, Walvoord) | Future, literal, after second advent | Future literal 1000 years on earth | Literal brief future loosing after 1000 years | Literal nations gathered against Jerusalem |
| Historic / classical premillennial (Peters, Pember, Govett, Panton, Lang) | Future, literal | Future literal earthly kingdom; partial rapture and reward themes added by Govett school | Brief literal post-millennial rebellion; probative purpose | Often the same Gog/Magog as Ezekiel; literal but symbolic of all opposition |
| Amillennial (Hendriksen, Hoeksema, Reformed) | Present, since the cross | Present church age | A future intensification of apostasy and antichrist before Christ's single return ([Christian Study Library](https://www.christianstudylibrary.org/article/doctrine-last-things-satans-little-season)) | Symbolic of all enemies of the church in the new covenant era |
| Postmillennial | Present or future progressive | Future golden age before Christ's return | A brief late apostasy before Christ's return ([Puritan Board thread](https://puritanboard.com/threads/postmillennial-hope-and-the-%E2%80%9Clittle-season%E2%80%9D.114465/)) | Symbolic |

**Purpose of the final rebellion.** The dominant exegesis across all four views is that the loosing demonstrates the radical depravity of the unregenerate human heart, even after either a thousand years of perfect righteous rule (premillennial) or millennia of gospel influence (amillennial / postmillennial). It is a final probative event vindicating divine judgment before the Great White Throne.

## Connection to conditional immortality, kingdom exclusion, and outer darkness

The themillennialkingdom.org.uk authors are noted for three closely related distinctives that *are* their actual signature teaching, distinct from anything labelled "little season":

1. **Partial rapture and millennial exclusion** (Govett, Panton, Lang, Chitwood). Only faithful believers participate in the first resurrection and the millennial reign; unfaithful but saved believers are excluded from the kingdom and consigned to "outer darkness" *during the millennium* — a realm of exclusion from kingdom privileges, not eternal damnation ([rationallychristian.org](https://www.rationallychristian.org/home/2020/12/24/a-christian-can-be-excluded-from-the-millennial-kingdom), [misthology.org PDF](https://misthology.org/pdf/articles/A_Panoramic_View_of_the_Outer_Darkness.pdf)).
2. **Reward and the judgment seat of Christ** (the bema). The faithful become co-heirs with Christ; the unfaithful retain salvation but lose rulership and inheritance.
3. **Kingdom-Church distinction**. The kingdom is a future earthly reign offered to faithful members of the present church, not the church itself.

The site's `TheDarknessOfTheOuter.htm` page is a compilation of Govett, Panton, and Lang on this theme, and explicitly does **not** reference Revelation 20:7-10 — its outer-darkness texts are Matthew 8:12, 22:13, and 25:30. There is no doctrinal bridge in this corpus from kingdom exclusion to a present "little season."

Conditional immortality (annihilationism) is a separate doctrine and is *not* the position of the themillennialkingdom.org.uk circle, which holds standard eternal punishment for the unsaved and eternal security for the saved.

## Whether it is connected to the themillennialkingdom.org.uk circle

**No.** The phrase "Little Season Eschatology" does not appear on themillennialkingdom.org.uk, in Lamp Broadcast publications, in the works of Panton, Govett, Lang, Pember, Peters, or Chitwood, or in the Govett-school journals. The fringe ministries that use the phrase as a label are independent of, and theologically incompatible with, the classical premillennial position the site exists to republish. If the user encountered the phrase in association with themillennialkingdom.org.uk, it is almost certainly a generic descriptive term ("eschatology that pays attention to the little season") rather than a proper noun framework, or it has been imported from outside the site's actual corpus.

- High confidence on the factual claim that "Little Season Eschatology" is not a recognised framework in classical premillennial scholarship. This is supported by the absence of the phrase across the entire Lamp Broadcast and themillennialkingdom.org.uk corpus, and by every Reformed and dispensational secondary source surveyed.
- High confidence on the description of the fringe usage. The Little Season Ministries site, the Rhising book, and the bloggers are explicit about their position.
- Medium confidence on the precise treatment by Pember and Peters of the little season. Their primary texts were not directly fetched for this brief; the characterisation is based on standard secondary summaries and the structure of their published systems. A direct search of *The Theocratic Kingdom* and *Earth's Earliest Ages* would tighten this if needed.
- Low confidence that there is *any* significant teacher in the themillennialkingdom.org.uk lineage who treats the little season as a doctrinal headline. None was found despite targeted searches.

## Actionable takeaways

1. **For modernising the themillennialkingdom.org.uk site:** treat "Little Season Eschatology" as outside the site's actual doctrinal heritage. The site's distinctives are *kingdom exclusion*, *partial rapture*, *reward at the bema*, and *outer darkness as a millennial-age realm*, not the little season. Any modernised navigation or topic taxonomy should foreground these.
2. **If the user intends to engage with the fringe Little Season movement:** position the site's classical authors as a corrective. Govett, Panton, Lang, Pember, Peters, and Chitwood all explicitly affirm a future literal millennium and reject any scheme in which Christ has already returned and reigned.
