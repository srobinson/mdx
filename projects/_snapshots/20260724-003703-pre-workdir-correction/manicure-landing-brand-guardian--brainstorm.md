# Manicure Landing: Brand Foundation

Brainstorm output for the brand-guardian lens of the Manicure landing-page workstream. Everything downstream (copy, layout, motion, typography, CTAs) should trace back to this document.

## 1. Brand Promise

Manicure intercepts every request your coding agent sends to Claude, shows you the payload, and lets you edit it before forwarding.

The promise is narrow by design. Manicure does not promise productivity, better code, or cost savings. It promises that the opaque layer between your agent and the model stops being opaque. What the developer does with the visibility is their own responsibility. This restraint is the whole point. A broader promise would contradict the diagnostic posture.

## 2. Primary Hero Claim

**Your agent sends more than you think.**

Why this one:

- Sets up the first scroll (demo or visceral reveal) without pre-specifying what will be shown.
- Sits close to accusation and still respectful. The developer is invited to be curious.
- Testable inside the first scroll. The visitor's own payload either confirms or denies the claim.
- No verbs of hype. No numbers in the hero itself. Numbers live below the fold, attached to live payload.
- Six words. The brand argues that fewer tokens means higher signal. The hero walks the thesis.

## 3. Anchor Words

**Forensic.** Manicure gathers evidence. It does not argue conclusions. The tool hands the developer the payload and lets them draw their own inference. The brand throughout should read as an incident report.

**Meticulous.** The product is named for a grooming craft defined by attention to small detail. Every token is a nail. Nothing is glossed. Copy that skips details to keep the scroll moving betrays the brand.

**Candid.** Willing to tell the developer something they do not want to hear: your context is polluted, your MCPs are noise, the agent you trust is wasting signal. Candor justifies the diagnostic posture.

**Dry.** No exclamation points. No adjectives stacked three deep. No "supercharge." Copy reads at the level of a technical changelog with slightly more intent. Stuart's voice is flat by design. The product speaks through specifics. Enthusiasm is forbidden.

**Spare.** The brand walks the thesis. Manicure argues that fewer tokens means higher signal. The landing page must ship fewer tokens than any competitor's and still say more. If the page itself is bloated, the thesis fails before the demo loads.

## 4. Voice Sample

> Your coding agent ships twenty tools with every request. You used two. Manicure shows you the eighteen you didn't.

Three sentences, 23 words. Specific. Confronting without contempt. No verbs of hype, no qualifying adjectives, no "should." The third sentence names what the product does by pointing at the problem. Features never appear.

## 5. What to NEVER Say

**"Supercharge your workflow."**
Manicure's thesis is that you already have too much. Any verb that implies addition contradicts the product. The brand adds visibility and removes tokens. Supercharging is forbidden by the thesis itself.

**"10x your productivity."**
The audience is Claude Code users. They have been inoculated against this phrasing by two years of AI marketing. Using it immediately puts Manicure in the pile of tools it is trying to distinguish itself from.

**"Save on API costs."**
Stuart has explicitly ruled this out. Framing Manicure as a cost tool reduces a diagnostic to an expense optimizer. The thesis is signal-to-noise ratio. Cost is a downstream effect.

**"The future of AI development."**
Any capital R rhetoric about futures or revolutions. Manicure operates in the present tense. Future talk implies the visitor needs to catch up. Manicure's stance is that the visitor is already doing the thing. The question is whether they know what it looks like.

**"Trusted by engineers at [logos]."**
Social proof via logo wall. The brand asks the visitor to distrust their own setup. Asking them to trust a logo wall in the same scroll contradicts the posture and reads as the exact move they came in expecting to dismiss.

## 6. Differentiator

Most dev tool landing pages argue for the product. Manicure argues against the visitor's current setup.

Every scroll on a standard dev tool page escalates commitment to the product: hero, features, social proof, CTA. Manicure inverts the axis. The scroll escalates doubt about the visitor's current workflow. The product is named, briefly demonstrated, and then named again at the bottom. It otherwise stays offstage while the visitor watches their own payload get dismantled. The visitor becomes the subject of the investigation. The product is the instrument.

## 7. Stance on "Education Not Sales"

Three concrete applications:

**CTAs invite looking.** The primary CTA reads "Inspect a real payload" or "Paste your last request." "Install Manicure" happens later, after the visitor has seen something. A visitor who inspects a payload and closes the tab has still been moved. That counts as a conversion in this brand.

**Every claim carries a specific measurement on the same line.** The page does not say "your context is bloated." It says "your last session shipped 47,000 tokens; Claude read 12,000." Abstractions are tolerated only when pinned to a live number. The demo is the proof. The copy is the annotation.

**The page is structured around the visitor's ignorance.** Section order: what you didn't know, how much of it there is, what the tool lets you do about it. The three pillars (Surface, Realize, Tamper) already encode this arc. Every section reinforces revelation. Features appear only as the resolution of a problem the visitor has already seen demonstrated.

## 8. On Shareable Overlays

Overlays fit the brand cleanly. A published overlay is a teaching artifact: install it, run it, watch what gets trimmed and what survives. Learning happens by observation. No manual required.

The community interpretation. Manicure users publish overlays to expose their own curation as evidence. "Here is what I strip from Python sessions. Here is why. Run it and see." The overlay library reads as a corpus of annotated professional judgment. A marketplace would break the brand.

Brand implication for the UI and landing-page treatment. If overlays ship, their surface should read like a library catalogue. No badges, no ratings, no "most popular." List the overlay, the author, and what it removes. Let the artifact speak.

## Downstream hooks

Other lenses working on this landing page should treat the following as load-bearing:

- The hero does not explain the product. The first scroll does.
- Copy density is a brand signal. A bloated page invalidates the thesis.
- Numbers are required wherever claims appear. Abstractions without a number on the same line are rejected.
- CTAs read as invitations to inspect. Install and signup live downstream of inspection.
- No logos, no testimonials, no trust badges. Social proof contradicts the diagnostic posture.
