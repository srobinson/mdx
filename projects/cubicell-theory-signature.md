# Lens 1: Constraints as Signature

## Position

Cubicell is a kinetic print medium built from solids. Its signature comes from a cube grammar that stays graphic while the scene moves through three dimensional space. Flat faces provide mass. Persistent edge bars provide ink. Black or white polarity provides the plate. Authored state, transport, and camera provide time.

**Persistent edges turn camera motion into authored graphic continuity that remains legible across every scale.**

That is the strongest artistic claim. Most three dimensional rendering uses distance, lighting, and material response to make geometry feel physical. Cubicell has none of those cues. Its minimum screen thickness preserves every visible edge as a designed mark. A camera retreat therefore changes composition and density while the drawing refuses to dissolve. The real cost is congestion: distant cells can accumulate into dark texture because their edges never become optically negligible.

## The constraint signatures

### Unlit geometry becomes moving print

`MeshBasicMaterial` gives every face and bar a flat value without lighting or normal based shading. The aesthetic is closer to cut paper, plotter ink, screen print, and technical illustration than rendered plastic. Rotation changes contour and overlap without changing the apparent light source. That stability lets motion carry the meaning because surface illumination cannot distract from it.

The cost is equally specific. Specular highlights, soft shadow, reflected color, surface grain, and atmospheric depth cannot explain a form. A piece must establish depth through occlusion, face color, edge rhythm, scale, camera movement, and temporal sequence. Work that embraces this becomes unusually direct: animated diagrams, impossible constructions, geometric emblems, and moving prints whose spatial logic is readable as notation.

### Persistent edges create scale invariant ink

The edge coverage layer enforces a minimum screen thickness. An edge remains present when its cube shrinks or recedes. This turns line weight into a property of the artwork rather than a side effect of projection. A large camera move can pass from one readable cell to a field of hundreds while preserving a continuous visual language.

The cost is a hard density ceiling. Distance does not provide automatic simplification, so crowded scenes can become crosshatched masses. Composition must use spacing, polarity, visibility, and camera timing to manage that accumulation. This limit produces a distinct class of scale studies: works where zoom changes a drawing from object, to pattern, to texture without ever becoming conventional perspective haze.

### Separate faces and bars create registered layers

Each cube is six face quads plus twelve edge bars. Faces own the silhouette, while bars sit over that mass as graphic decoration. The result behaves like two registered print layers: a field of flat solids with a contour system laid on top. Edge changes can describe orientation, emphasis, or rhythm without changing the underlying body.

The cost is that edge treatment cannot create a truly rounded solid. With no corner geometry, round and chamfer deformation reads cleanly only through `shapeSize` 0.4. Above that threshold, independently deformed bars meet as lumps, wedges, and spikes. The native aesthetic is therefore constructed and jointed. Moderate softening can tune the mark, while large organic fillets contradict the actual topology.

Work unique to this constraint should expose the joint grammar. Corners can behave like punctuation, intersections like registration marks, and repeated edge treatments like a score. Hiding those joints in pursuit of seamless sculpture would spend complexity while erasing a recognizable signature.

### Black or white polarity makes every piece reversible

Scene polarity restricts the environment to black or white. Combined with unlit material, this gives a piece the logic of a positive or negative plate. Inversion can change emotional temperature while preserving the same spatial construction and line hierarchy.

The cost is the absence of environmental mood as a continuous control. Atmosphere cannot come from a sunset, studio light, photographic backdrop, or textured world. Mood must come from polarity, color relationships, density, pace, and camera behavior. That constraint supports a coherent body of work: pieces can differ radically in motion and structure while remaining visibly related at first glance.

### A flat cell array makes repetition the compositional engine

Cells are peers in a flat array. There is no recursion and no embedded media. Instancing makes repeated face and edge geometry the efficient native unit. This encourages serial composition: grids, swarms, lattices, accumulations, permutations, and coordinated fields.

The cost is the lack of nested symbols, recursive worlds, image collage, and video surfaces. Complexity must remain explicit as cells and states. That makes authorship legible. A viewer can learn the vocabulary, then notice how repetition, adjacency, omission, and timing alter its meaning. Stickiness comes from mastery of a finite instrument rather than novelty imported from arbitrary media.

### Captured camera turns viewpoint into authored material

Morph channels, transport, and camera captured per state make a piece a sequence of views rather than a model waiting to be inspected. The camera can reveal, conceal, flatten, or invert a construction at deliberate moments. A state change can coordinate object transformation with the observer's movement.

This is where the spinor material in `THEORY.md` becomes artistically useful. A final orientation does not contain the history of the path that reached it. Cubicell can make that missing history visible through a scored sequence of camera poses and morph states. A loop may return a cube to an apparently identical pose while its journey remains the content.

The system does not calculate homotopy class or preserve a spinor phase. Any distinction between a 360 degree and 720 degree path must be authored through visible state, timing, color, or camera choreography. That cost prevents the mathematics from becoming an automatic effect. The artist must construct the proof as an experience.

## Sources of stickiness

### Recognition

Flat mass, persistent ink, explicit joints, and binary polarity form a stable visual fingerprint. The cost of this coherence is a risk of sameness. Each piece must earn variation through composition, color, motion, and camera because lighting and media cannot supply it cheaply.

### Replay

Transport and captured camera make path dependent work possible. A still frame cannot exhaust a piece whose meaning lies in how it arrived there. The cost is that temporal authorship must be precise. Weak timing leaves only a static cube arrangement, and the medium has few decorative effects to disguise it.

### Mastery

Six faces and twelve bars create a finite grammar that viewers can learn. Per instance deformation, morph channels, cell arrangement, and camera state create depth inside that grammar. The cost is discipline: novelty must emerge from combinations of known parts. That pressure is valuable because repeated viewing reveals decisions rather than assets.

## Work this medium can own

1. **Rotation proofs.** A cube, its face and edge marks, state morphs, and an authored camera can make rotational history perceptible. The absence of lighting keeps attention on path and orientation. The cost is manual encoding of every conceptual distinction.

2. **Scale scores.** Camera movement can turn a few cells into a dense field while minimum edge coverage preserves continuity. The work can cross object, pattern, and texture in one movement. The cost is active control of visual congestion.

3. **Kinetic prints.** Black or white polarity, flat faces, and contour bars can produce animations that retain the authority of a printed plate. The cost is a narrow atmospheric palette, which makes rhythm and composition carry more weight.

4. **Cellular choreography.** Instanced peer cells can form ensembles where identity comes from coordinated variation rather than a hero object. The cost is the flat structure: hierarchy must be performed through scale, position, visibility, and timing.

5. **Joint studies.** The clean deformation range through 0.4 permits controlled shifts among sharp, round, and chamfered marks while retaining an engineered corner language. The cost above that range is visible interpenetration, which establishes a real stylistic boundary.

## Artistic direction

The coherent destination is a school of kinetic diagrams: authored camera paths moving through flat, persistent, cellular drawings whose histories matter more than their final poses. Cubicell should be judged by the clarity and surprise it produces inside that grammar. Naturalism, seamless organic sculpture, media collage, and recursive world building carry concrete topology and representation costs while weakening the signature already present.

The medium becomes most distinctive when a viewer can recognize a Cubicell piece from one frame, then discover through playback that the frame was only one point on a meaningful path.
