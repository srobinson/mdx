## Cross-report contradictions outside the ten axes

1. **Pinned-baseline health dispute.** Boundary map reports `credential_refresh.py:83` as a `SyntaxError` verified via `py_compile` (addon cannot load at `a252df24`); research program repeats a parse failure for `credential_source.py:184`; topology options re-verified with the repo's own interpreter (`requires-python >= 3.14`, PEP 758 permits unparenthesized multi-exception clauses) and found the flagged files parse clean, attributing the failure to an older ambient interpreter. Topology's method is stronger. Adjudication: treat the syntax-defect claims as retracted; do not carry "tm was broken at the pin" as evidence anywhere downstream. The zero-dependency rule does not need it.
2. **"Raw" is not wire octets.** Mechanics establishes tm's `request.raw`/`response.raw` are decoded UTF-8 text, not socket bytes; several other reports use "byte-faithful raw capture" loosely. Adjudication: adopt mechanics' precise evidence vocabulary (`decoded_provider_body_bytes` vs true wire octets) in every downstream contract; never claim wire-octet fidelity the mechanism does not provide.
3. **Launch-wrapper coverage vs background agents.** Boundary map/taxonomy place interposition at the launcher env seam; protocol research R21/R25 documents that Claude Code's background supervisor ignores PATH wrappers and can miss shell-exported env, recommending `~/.claude/settings.json` enrollment — which collides with D8 (keep native homes, no home mutation). Under lilo the env is set on the spawned process, not a shell, so inheritance may suffice; nobody has proven it. Unresolved; experiment listed below. Coverage honesty (R17–R19: base-URL capture sees inference only, not fast-mode checks, WebFetch preflight, telemetry, or cloud surfaces) must be enumerated in product copy regardless.
4. **Streaming buffer bound.** tm's tee accumulates whole responses in memory (crash loses everything, reject F6); security/durability wants bounded disk spool with sync-per-chunk-batch; taxonomy wants tee-never-buffer. All reject the tm shape; the spool-vs-sync latency budget is unmeasured (axis 2 experiment covers it).
5. **CAPTURE column vocabulary.** Product contract fixes session-view states as `active/complete/lost/failed` with no `none`; security/durability's state machine adds `Preparing/Ready/Draining` and exchange-level states. Not contradictory, but two granularities; the session-facing summary and the internal state machine must be defined together once.

## Enterprise acceptance evidence

What makes mandatory capture sellable, per the corpus, and what a buyer will probe:

- **The claim**: every agent action on this host is evidenced by construction — who authorized it (identity audit in the spawn transaction), what was sent, what came back, whether a human touched it. No opt-in capture model can make this claim (user value §5).
- **Primitives already proven in the lab**: canonical-JSON digests, content addressing, immutable-on-create evidence rows, attributed action audit, launcher-minted identity ("a caller is its run").
- **Gating gaps buyers will probe**: retention/deletion story, payload redaction, secret never-capture proof, access authorization and audited reads. All four are v1-or-enterprise-tier requirements in the adjudication above.
- **Legal boundary (B5/R37/U10)**: mandatory capture of developer traffic is employee monitoring in the EU sense; consent is not the available basis; works-council co-determination attaches to the system. Resolve whether littleorgans *enables* monitoring (deployer's obligation) or *performs* it before the word "mandatory" appears in user-facing copy.
- **Coverage honesty**: "mandatory capture of model inference", with the R17 non-covered host list published. The alternative is a false completeness claim.
- **Acceptance harness**: security/durability's hostile-workload and crash matrices plus product contract's 12 release-gate assertions are the enterprise qualification suite; test evidence maps ~60 tm tests worth recreating and 6 gaps littleorgans must author fresh.

## Governing finding

The thirteen reports agree on far more than they dispute — filesystem-authoritative two-tier storage, Claude-first redirect interposition, observation-only v1, `SessionId` as the sole spine, Rust-in-workspace packaging, and the delete list. The one live design contradiction is the hot-path durability boundary: best-effort persistence that never blocks the wire (user value, taxonomy) versus a durable request acknowledgement before provider release (mechanics, security/durability). It is the single most expensive decision to get wrong because it is irreversible (a fire-and-forget path cannot gain a barrier later), and it is cheap to resolve with one measured crash-and-latency spike. Secondary headline: vendor documentation showing Codex honors a base URL override may collapse the entire TLS-interception axis, removing the hardest engineering and trust program from scope; one probe decides it.
